What is Client Segmentation?
----------------------------
Client segmentation is "the process of dividing customers into groups based on shared characteristics or needs of different customer groups," so that the business operations team or customer success manager team can provide different types of services to different customer groups (e.g., manual/human services or product services).

Two Types of Client Segmentation
---------------------------------

WHO THEY ARE (Segmenting clients based on who they are)
Examples: age, geography, urban or rural, income, relationship status, family, job category

These are person-based customer labels. Applied to our contracted entities (corporations), examples include:
  - Years since company registration
  - Industry/sector the company operates in
  - Company size
  - Company revenue
  - Company registration location

WHAT THEY DO (Segmenting clients based on behaviors)
Behavior-based client segmentation varies in emphasis depending on different companies' business operation models, but generally we have the following behavioral segmentation dimensions:
1. Customer balance scale in ad accounts
2. Customer overall and time-period-based spend/consumption
3. Customer retention duration and retention status
4. Long-term loyalty (customer spend situation and retention situation)

Different service approaches should be adopted based on strategic marketing and demand positioning suited to specific customer groups.

How Should SaaS Service Companies Develop Client Segmentation Strategies?
==========================================================================

Core Framework
--------------

Importance of SaaS Client Segmentation
1. Personalized marketing, customized communication, improving customer group relevance and engagement.
2. Identifying needs and pain points, building features and solutions that meet specific needs.
3. Identifying high-risk and **high-value** segments, providing targeted retention strategies.

SaaS Customer Segmentation Types
----------------------------------
1. Demographic: e.g., company industry, company size, geographic region, company age... Assumption: customers with the same characteristics also have the same expectations/needs. (Needs to be checked) (Ad delivery country/region/category *)

2. Firmographic, B2B SaaS context: e.g., industry, size, number of employees, revenue...

3. Need-based: Company's expected outcomes, pain points to advance, goals...

4. *Value-based: e.g., Total Growth Value (incremental spend, multi-media multi-ad-account); Stable Earned Annual Recurring Revenue (eARR); Potential Annual Recurring Revenue from additional services not yet purchased (wARR, key to measuring growth); Depth and breadth of product feature usage, especially usage of key features tied to core value (Product adoption rate: customer stickiness); LTV (Customer Lifetime Value): total net revenue over the entire duration of the business relationship (spend amount + retention duration); MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue): predictable, recurring total revenue obtained from subscription customers each month, one of the most core health metrics. Churn rate (Customer Churn Rate): the percentage of customers who stop spending or churn within a specific period. ****

5. Psychographic (delta)

6. Technographic: device type, platform or operating system, browser... (to review)

How to Do Client Segmentation for SaaS Products?
--------------------------------------------------
1. Define client segmentation goals (**using the SMART method**) (Customer lifecycle (beginner + mature + decline) / (high potential / low potential))
2. Thoroughly analyze existing customer wide table data.
3. Based on defined goals, search for insights, ultimately leading to matching product services.

B2B and B2C Client Segmentation
=================================

B2B (*)
-------
B2B business processes typically involve multiple decision factors, longer business cycles, and rational, data-driven, ROI-centered business decisions. For these reasons, B2B companies may use firmographics, purchasing behavior, decision factors, customer needs, lifecycle stages, and technology stacks as segmentation models.

B2C
---
B2C business decisions are typically driven by individuals or families based on factors such as emotions, convenience, reputation, personal preferences, or trends. Because of this, B2C customer success teams may use demographics, psychographics, geographic location, behavioral data, and needs and interests to segment customers.

How HubSpot Does Client Segmentation
======================================

Pain Point
-----------
HubSpot's customer base is extremely large, ranging from individuals running personal blogs to large enterprises with thousands of employees -- all using their products. Initially, their sales and customer service teams were struggling because they couldn't possibly serve a free user and a major client paying hundreds of thousands of dollars annually in the same way. Without segmentation, not only would resources be wasted, but the customer experience on both sides would suffer.

Segmentation Dimensions
-------------------------

"Customer Value" Segmentation (Tier-Based Segmentation)
Look at how much value a customer can bring, or how much potential they have.
- High-value customers (High-Touch): For example, Fortune 500 large enterprises that pay the most and have the greatest potential.
- Medium-value customers (Low-Touch): Growing mid-size companies with some level of payment, needing some support.
- Mass-market customers (Tech-Touch): Small companies or free users who pay very little, extremely large in number.

"Product Usage Behavior" Segmentation (Behavioral Segmentation)
Looking at customer size alone is not enough; you also need to see how customers actually use the product.
- Deep users: Not only use their core marketing tools but also purchased their sales and customer service tools, deeply integrated.
- Single-function users: May only use their email sending feature, ignoring other functions.
- Low-activity users: Registered but rarely log in, at risk of churning.

Combined Dimensions & Service Strategies
------------------------------------------
For high-value + deep-usage customers: They assign their most elite Customer Success Managers (CSMs) to provide "one-on-one" concierge service, hold regular strategy meetings, and proactively help customers plan how to achieve greater success.

For medium-value customers: They adopt a "one-to-many" model. For example, one CSM manages several hundred customers, providing help through regular online training sessions and sharing meetings rather than one-on-one chats.

For mass-market customers: They rely entirely on "automation and self-service." They solve customer problems through massive online help documentation, intelligent chatbots, and auto-triggered onboarding emails.

Benefits
---------
- Resources are used where they matter most: Elite CSMs serve the most important customers, with extremely high efficiency.
- Customer experience improved: Large clients feel valued, small clients can quickly find answers -- each gets what they need.
- Revenue increased: Through deep service for high-value customers, they more easily sold additional products (cross-selling and value-added services), and customer retention rates also improved.
